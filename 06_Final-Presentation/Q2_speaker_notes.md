## Title

> Kia ora, we're Group 8, while True: learn(). I'm Andrew, with Harish and Niwesh. In ten minutes we'll show what thirty-six years of building consents tell us about the cost of building, our kinds of building market, and whether we can see next year coming.

## Slide 1 · Why look at building consents?

> Before anything is built, a council issues a building consent, and Stats NZ has recorded every one, by district, every month since 1990. So consents are an early signal. Here are the five dearest and the five cheapest districts to build in. In 2025 a square metre of new house cost about 4,600 dollars in Queenstown and about 2,700 in Selwyn. That gap is where our story starts.

## Slide 2 · From 10 million rows to a fair comparison

> The raw data is over ten million rows in thirteen tables. We added the months up into one row per district per quarter or year. Questions two and three leave out the Chatham Islands: only 34 new homes in ten years. The key step was removing inflation. In the dollars of each year, a square metre of new house costs almost five times what it did in 1991. After removing building-cost inflation, the rise is about twenty percent. Harish will now explain our questions.

## Slide 7 · Three kinds of building market

> Our second question: what kinds of building market does New Zealand have? We described each of 66 districts by nine measures over the last ten years: the kinds of home it builds, how many for its population, whether building is growing, how much it swings year to year, its cost, house size, and how much is commercial. A grouping method called k-means put districts that build alike together. Each dot is a district. Close dots build alike, and the dashed outlines show where most of each kind sit. House-building districts, like Selwyn and Tasman, build many big stand-alone houses. Slow provincial districts, like Gore and Grey, build little and unevenly, and are the cheapest. Urban markets, the cities and Queenstown, build the most townhouses and are the dearest.

## Slide 8 · How the markets differ, and how sure we are

> This chart shows the middle district of each kind. In urban markets about four in ten new homes are townhouses or apartments, against one in ten elsewhere, and building costs five percent more. House-building districts build the most homes per resident. Slow provincial districts are about eight percent cheaper, and commercial building makes up the biggest share of what they build. Why three groups? Two or three fit the data about equally well, but three held up best when we repeated the grouping on a hundred re-drawn samples, and each group is one we can name. How sure are we? A second method put 60 of the 66 districts in the same kind. But the edges are soft. A grouping has no right answer to test against, and Palmerston North and Tauranga sit between two kinds. Niwesh will now look ahead to next year.
